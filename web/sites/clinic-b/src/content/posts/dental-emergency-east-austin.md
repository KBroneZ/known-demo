When something goes wrong with a tooth, a few calm steps can make a real difference. This is general information, not a replacement for professional advice. If you have trouble breathing or swallowing, bleeding you cannot control, or swelling that spreads to your face or neck, go to an emergency room or call 911.

## Sudden toothache

Rinse your mouth with warm water and use floss to remove anything caught between the teeth. A cold compress on the outside of the cheek can help with swelling. Follow the label on any over-the-counter pain reliever. Pain that lasts more than a day, or comes with fever or swelling, needs a prompt exam. The ADA's [MouthHealthy page on dental emergencies](https://www.mouthhealthy.org/all-topics-a-z/dental-emergencies) lists common cases and what to do.

## A cracked or broken tooth

Rinse with warm water and keep any pieces you find. A cold compress helps with swelling. If a sharp edge is cutting your tongue or cheek, a little dental wax or sugar-free gum can cover it until you are seen.

## A knocked-out adult tooth

Handle the tooth by the crown, not the root. Rinse it gently if it is dirty, and put it back in the socket if you can. If not, keep it in milk. Then call us on your way. Do not try to replace a baby tooth.

## A lost crown or filling

Keep the crown and bring it with you. The tooth underneath is exposed, so it needs to be seen soon.
