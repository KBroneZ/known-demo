Book an earlier appointment instead of waiting for your next yearly checkup. A cavity does not heal on its own, and a small one is usually simpler for your dentist to treat than a large one.

## Why cavities between teeth should not wait

A cavity is a spot where decay has damaged the tooth. Left alone, it can grow deeper. Over time it can reach the inner part of the tooth, which can mean more pain and more involved treatment.

Cavities between teeth are easy to miss. You cannot see the space between two teeth well in a mirror, and a small cavity there may cause no pain at all. If you have noticed one, or something that feels like one, that is a good reason to get it looked at soon.

Your dentist will examine the tooth and decide what it needs. Some cavities need a filling. Others may need a different approach. That decision belongs to your dentist after an exam, not to an online article.

## Can a cavity show up after a recent checkup?

Many patients ask how their teeth can look worse so soon after a visit. There are a few possible reasons.

- Cavities between teeth are hard to see, even for a dentist, without X-rays.
- A very small cavity may not have been large enough to find at your last visit.
- What you noticed may be a different problem, such as food catching between teeth or a rough edge.

None of this means you did something wrong by going to your checkup. It just means your dentist should look at what you found. We take X-rays only when your dentist needs them, and a cavity between teeth is one reason they might.

## What to do until your visit

A few simple habits help while you wait for your appointment:

- Brush twice a day with fluoride toothpaste.
- Clean between your teeth once a day, gently, with floss or another between-teeth cleaner. [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/dentalhealth.html) has plain-language information on brushing, flossing and dental checkups.
- Cut back on sugary snacks and drinks.
- Do not poke at the area with a pin, toothpick or other sharp tool.

## When to call a dentist

Call right away, and do not wait for a routine slot, if you have any of these:

- Tooth pain that is strong, keeps coming back or wakes you at night
- Swelling in your gum, cheek or jaw
- A bad taste or pus near a tooth
- A broken tooth or a filling that fell out
- Pain when you bite down

If swelling spreads to your face or neck, or you have trouble breathing or swallowing, go to an emergency room.
