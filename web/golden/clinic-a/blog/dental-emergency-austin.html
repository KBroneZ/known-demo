What to Do in a Dental Emergency in Austin

By Dr. Elena Marsh · Reviewed by Dr. Marcus Oyelaran
Published October 5, 2026 · Updated October 14, 2026

Dental emergencies rarely happen at a convenient time. Knowing the first steps can protect a tooth while you get to a dentist. This is general information and not a substitute for professional advice. If you have trouble breathing or swallowing, uncontrolled bleeding or swelling that spreads to your face or neck, go to an emergency room or call 911.

A tooth is knocked out

Time matters. Pick the tooth up by the crown, the part you normally see, and avoid touching the root. If it is dirty, rinse it gently with water. Do not scrub it. If you can, place it back in the socket and hold it there. If you cannot, keep it moist in milk and get to a dentist as quickly as possible. Call us before you leave so we can prepare. Baby teeth should not be put back in the socket, so call the dentist for guidance.

A severe toothache

Rinse your mouth with warm water and gently floss to remove anything stuck between the teeth. Cold compresses on the outside of the cheek can ease swelling. Over-the-counter pain relievers can help until your visit, so follow the label directions. Do not place aspirin directly against the gum. Pain that lasts more than a day or two, or comes with fever or swelling, needs a prompt exam. The ADA’s MouthHealthy page on dental emergencies lists common cases and what to do.

A chipped or broken tooth

Rinse with warm water and save any pieces. Use a cold compress for swelling. If the edge is sharp, a piece of sugar-free gum or dental wax can protect your tongue until you are seen.

A lost filling or crown

Keep the crown if you can. A dental cement from a pharmacy can hold it in place temporarily, but a dentist needs to see it soon, since the tooth underneath is exposed.
