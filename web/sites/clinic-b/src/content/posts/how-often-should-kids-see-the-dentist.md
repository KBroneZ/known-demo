Parents often ask how soon a child should see a dentist and how often after that. The usual answer is a checkup about twice a year, starting around the first birthday or when the first tooth appears. That is a good starting point, not a rule, and some children benefit from a different rhythm.

## Why twice a year is the default

Baby teeth and new adult teeth change quickly. A visit every six months is often frequent enough to spot a small problem early and to see how teeth and jaws are growing. At each visit we count teeth, check the gums and look at how the bite is developing.

## When a child may need to come more often

Your child's dentist may suggest closer visits if your child has:

- A recent cavity or several cavities.
- Braces, aligners or other appliances that make cleaning harder.
- Habits such as thumb sucking that may affect how the teeth come in.
- Difficulty brushing alone, for example because of a medical condition.

Every child is different, so the schedule is set by the dentist after an exam rather than assumed in advance. The ADA's patient site, [MouthHealthy](https://www.mouthhealthy.org/), has plain-language tips for daily care at home between visits.
